## SCENE 1: COLD OPEN - "Something Is Following You"
**Script lines 1-3 | ~25 seconds | Arc: COLD OPEN**

### VO Text
> Something is following you. Right now, as you watch this, a number you have never seen... is quietly deciding what your life looks like. Not your credit score -- you know about that one. This is something else. Something hidden.

### 1. VISUAL
- **Primary:** Generated/stock imagery -- classified government document with redacted passages, harsh institutional lighting. Slow zoom in (5%/sec) on redacted numeric data.
- **B-roll option:** Cut to brief clips from **60 Minutes** (data broker overview footage) at ~8 sec mark for visual variety.
- **Color grade:** Cold/desaturated. Atmosphere: subtle fog overlay.

### 2. TEXT ON SCREEN
- None. Let the voice carry. The mystery is the hook.

### 3. VOICE PACING
- **SLOW to MEDIUM.** Deliberate pauses between phrases. The "[BEAT]" after "that one" is critical -- hold it. This is a whisper-in-your-ear tone. The audience needs to lean in.

### 4. MUSIC
- **Tense/ominous.** Low drone, sub-bass. No melody yet. Think single sustained note with slight dissonance. Barely there -- the silence is doing the work.

### 5. SFX
- Subtle tension hit on the first word "Something." Low rumble, not a stinger.
- Paper rustling or document handling sounds underneath (very low in mix).

### 6. ENERGY LEVEL
- **LOW.** This is the quiet before everything. Contrast will come from Scene 2.

## SCENE 2: COLD OPEN ESCALATION - "They Have Heard of You"
**Script lines 3 | ~30 seconds | Arc: COLD OPEN**

### VO Text
> And the companies that created it? You've never heard of them. But they have heard of you. They know where you've lived, who you've lived with, what you've bought, what you owe -- and what you did on a Tuesday in 2014. They have assembled a portrait of you so detailed it would make a stalker blush... and they have reduced that portrait to a single number. A secret score.

### 1. VISUAL
- **Primary:** Generated/stock -- data broker consumer score report document with numeric risk assessment. Slow zoom on score number.
- **B-roll at ~10 sec:** Cut to **John Oliver** clip (data broker segment) showing data broker corporate imagery. 3-5 seconds.
- **B-roll at ~20 sec:** Cut to **60 Minutes** clip showing data on screens / broker office.

### 2. TEXT ON SCREEN
- At the words "A secret score" -- type on screen: **"A SECRET SCORE"** (typewriter style, center frame, hold 2 sec).

### 3. VOICE PACING
- **MEDIUM building to FAST.** The list of "what they know" should accelerate slightly -- building momentum. Then SLAM the brakes on "A secret score." Hold the pause after.

### 4. MUSIC
- Drone continues, add a slow pulse. Building tension. Still no melody.

### 5. SFX
- Impact hit on "A secret score." Sharp, clean, low-frequency thud. This is the first real punctuation mark.

### 6. ENERGY LEVEL
- **LOW to MEDIUM.** Rising from Scene 1. The energy comes from vocal delivery, not visuals.

## SCENE 5: MARY LOUIS - Setup

> Mary Louis paid her rent on time for sixteen years. Sixteen years. Not a single missed payment, not a single late check -- not once. She was, by every reasonable measure, the kind of tenant any landlord should dream of. So when she applied for a new apartment, she expected what anyone would expect. Approval.

### 1. VISUAL

### 2. TEXT ON SCREEN
- On "sixteen years": **"16 YEARS -- ZERO MISSED PAYMENTS"** (lower third, clean sans-serif).

### 3. VOICE PACING
- **MEDIUM.** Conversational, warm. Building a portrait of a real person. The repetition of "sixteen years" should land with emphasis but not aggression.

### 4. MUSIC
- Gentle, neutral underscore. Slightly warm. Piano or ambient pad. We are building empathy.

### 5. SFX
- None.

### 6. ENERGY LEVEL
- **LOW-MEDIUM.** Storytelling mode. The audience is being set up for the gut punch.

## SCENE 6: MARY LOUIS - The Rejection
**Script lines 17-21 | ~35 seconds | Arc: EMOTIONAL PEAK**

### VO Text
> She got a rejection letter. [long pause] No explanation. No human being she could call. Just a form that said, essentially... no. And the thing that denied her wasn't a person. It wasn't a landlord who looked at her application and made a judgment call. It was a number -- generated by a company she had never heard of, using data she had never been asked to verify. A ghost in the machine said Mary Louis wasn't good enough. Sixteen years of proof said otherwise. The ghost won.

### 1. VISUAL
- **Primary:** Hands holding a rejection letter or official denial form. Close-up, shallow depth of field. Slow zoom.
- **At "ghost in the machine":** Cut to abstract/dark visual -- server room, algorithmic code on screen. Use clip from **60 Minutes** (data broker tech footage) or **Incogni DeleteMe** (data broker explainer footage).

### 2. TEXT ON SCREEN
- On "rejection letter": **"DENIED"** stamp effect, red, center frame. Quick flash (1 sec).
- On "The ghost won": **"THE GHOST WON."** -- lower third, hold through pause.

### 3. VOICE PACING
- **SLOW.** "She got a rejection letter" is delivered flat, matter-of-fact. The pause after is where the emotional weight lives. Build intensity through the back half. "The ghost won" is quiet, final.

### 4. MUSIC
- Drops to near-silence on "rejection letter." Slowly builds a low, unsettling drone through the explanation. No resolution.

### 5. SFX
- Impact/thud on "rejection letter."
- Subtle digital glitch or static on "ghost in the machine."

### 6. ENERGY LEVEL
- **MEDIUM to HIGH.** Contrast from the warmth of Scene 5. This is the first real emotional hit.

## SCENE 8: "WHO BUILT THE GHOST?" - SafeRent Reveal
**Script lines 25-31 | ~30 seconds | Arc: TENSION BUILD / REVELATION**

### VO Text
> So who built the ghost? [...] The company is called SafeRent, formerly known as CoreLogic. Their product is a tenant screening score... SafeRent's algorithm doesn't just look at your rental history. It pulls from an enormous web of data -- court records, eviction filings, debt records -- and crunches all of it into a score.

### 1. VISUAL
- **"Who built the ghost?":** Dark, architectural imagery -- blueprints or shadowed figure. Hold 2 sec.
- **SafeRent explanation:** Composite visual showing data sources feeding into algorithm. Court documents, eviction filings, debt records flowing into a funnel/score.
- **B-roll:** **Data Broker Defends** clip (a data broker explaining their process). 4-5 sec here is perfect -- lets the audience see a real industry figure.

### 2. TEXT ON SCREEN
- **"SAFERENT (fka CoreLogic)"** -- entity label, lower third.
- **"TENANT SCREENING SCORE"** -- appears below company name.
- Visual data flow: "COURT RECORDS + EVICTION FILINGS + DEBT RECORDS = SCORE"

### 3. VOICE PACING
- **"Who built the ghost?"** is SLOW, dramatic. Pause.
- Company explanation is MEDIUM -- informational. Accelerates slightly through the data list.

### 4. MUSIC
- Building. Add rhythmic elements under the data explanation. Ticking or pulsing.

### 5. SFX
- Tension hit on "Who built the ghost?"
- Subtle digital/processing sounds during data list.

### 6. ENERGY LEVEL
- **MEDIUM, building.** We are entering investigative mode.

## SCENE 19: KYLE BEHM - "The Story That Haunts Me"
**Script lines 85-103 | ~90 seconds | Arc: CONTEXT SETUP to EMOTIONAL PEAK**

**NOTE: This is the longest and most emotionally demanding scene. Handle with care.**

### VO Text
> Now I need to tell you about Kyle Behm. Because his story is the one that haunts me. [...] Kyle was a young man who struggled with bipolar disorder. [...] every application included a personality test [...] Kyle failed every single one. Not because he couldn't do the work. [...] And Kyle Behm attempted suicide.

### 1. VISUAL
- **Kyle intro:** Portrait, documentary style. Warm lighting. Slow, respectful.
- **Personality test description:** Stock -- someone filling out a questionnaire form. Close-up on bubbled answers, hands holding pen.
- **B-roll:** **Algorithms Disability** clip. This is THE clip for this scene. Use 6-8 seconds showing how AI/algorithms discriminate against people with disabilities in hiring. This is the clip's exact subject matter.
- **At "attempted suicide":** Cut to black. Hold black screen for the full 1.2-second pause.
- **After pause:** Slowly fade up -- NOT a snap cut -- to next visual.

### 2. TEXT ON SCREEN
- **"KYLE BEHM"** -- person ID, typewriter reveal.
- During personality test description: **"PERSONALITY TEST = ILLEGAL MENTAL HEALTH SCREENING?"** (lower third, question mark intentional).
- **"KRONOS"** -- company label when father investigates.
- At "attempted suicide": NO TEXT. Black screen. Silence.

### 3. VOICE PACING
- **SLOW throughout.** This is not a section to rush. The "attempted suicide" line must be delivered quietly, almost gently. No emphasis, no drama. The fact itself is enough.
- The pause after is SACRED. Do not fill it with music, SFX, or visuals.

### 4. MUSIC
- Warm, empathetic underscore for setup. Fades as story darkens.
- **SILENCE on "attempted suicide" and the full pause after.** No music. This silence is the most important editorial choice in the video.
- Music returns slowly, gently, for the father's investigation.

### 5. SFX
- None during the emotional peak.
- Subtle tension sound when Kronos is named.

### 6. ENERGY LEVEL
- **MEDIUM building to the most EMOTIONALLY INTENSE moment in the video, then DROP to SILENCE.** The contrast between the fullness of Kyle's story and the emptiness of the black screen is the most powerful beat.

8. **Senate FTC** + **Transforming FTC** + **Consumer Privacy** -- Regulatory sections (Scenes 7, 9, 20, 24)
9. **Incogni DeleteMe** -- Data persistence/correction (Scenes 6, 14, 25)
10. **Uber Drivers** -- Algorithmic control over workers (Scene 17)

### Color Grade Strategy
- **Cold/desaturated:** Scenes 1-3 (cold open), all "system" scenes
- **Warm/archival:** Personal story setups (Mary, Carmen, Kyle, Diane intros)
- **Neutral/institutional:** Legal/regulatory scenes
- **Dark/high contrast:** Emotional peaks and revelations

### Key Editorial Principles
- **Silence is a tool.** The three most important silences: after "A secret score" (Scene 2), after "attempted suicide" (Scene 19), after "Break it" (Scene 29). Do not fill them.
- **B-roll clips should never exceed 6-8 seconds.** Cut back to primary visuals. The clips are evidence, not the story.
- **Text cards are earned.** Only the lines marked for full-screen text treatment get it. Do not add more.
- **The energy curve:** LOW (1-4) > MEDIUM (5-7) > HIGH (8-11) > LOW reset (12) > HIGH (13-15) > MEDIUM (16-17) > HIGH (18) > EMOTIONAL PEAK (19) > MEDIUM (20) > HIGH (21-22) > MEDIUM (23-24) > HIGH (25-26) > WARM (27) > HIGH (28) > SILENCE (29).
