Crispy on the outside… gooey in the middle. These are the only chocolate chip cookies I make anymore — and there are three little things that make them turn out like this every single time. Let's go.
<break time="1.0s" />
Here's everything you need: butter, brown sugar, white sugar, one egg plus an extra yolk, vanilla, flour, baking soda, salt — and way too much chocolate. Full amounts are on screen at the end, so don't write anything down.
<break time="1.0s" />
Rule number one: brown your butter. Melt it on medium and just… keep swirling. It'll foam up — then go quiet, and then… you'll see little golden-brown bits at the bottom. <break time="1.5s" /> Pull it right there — and let it cool for ten minutes so it doesn't scramble the eggs.
<break time="1.0s" />
Both sugars into the butter — mostly brown, that's where the chewy comes from. Whisk it a minute till it looks like caramel. Then rule number two: one whole egg… and one extra yolk. That yolk is the gooey center. Vanilla — and whisk till it's glossy and thick.
<break time="1.0s" />
Flour, baking soda, salt. Fold — don't beat — <break time="1.5s" /> just until you can't see dry flour. Overmixing is how you get tough cookies.
<break time="1.0s" />
Now the chocolate. Half chips… and half a chopped bar. The chunks melt into puddles, the chips hold their shape. Fold it in.
<break time="1.0s" />
Big scoops — like three-tablespoons big. And rule number three: chill them. Thirty minutes minimum. Cold dough spreads slower — edges crisp, middle stays soft.
<break time="1.0s" />
Three-seventy-five, ten to twelve minutes. Watch through the door — golden edges, and a middle that still looks a little underdone. Out they come — tap the tray once… flaky salt… and now the hardest part: leave them alone for five minutes.
<break time="1.0s" />
…okay. Look at that. <break time="2.0s" /> Crispy edge. Gooey middle. Every time.
