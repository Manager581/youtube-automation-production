## DETAILED COMPANY PROFILES

### 1. CoreLogic / Cotality

- **Ownership**: Acquired by **Stone Point Capital** and **Insight Partners** for **$5.9 billion** ($80/share cash) on February 4, 2021. Taken private, delisted from NYSE.
- **Revenue**: Last public full-year revenue was **$1.6 billion** (2020). Now privately held; some trackers estimate ~$5B though unconfirmed.
- **Rebranding**: Renamed to **Cotality** on March 24, 2025, with tagline "Intelligence beyond bounds."
- **Acquisition history**: 23 total acquisitions. Peak years: 2017 (4 acquisitions), 2018 and 2021 (3 each). Most recent: Prime Ecosystem (insurance claim management software, January 2025).
- **Tenant screening**: CoreLogic Credco provides consumer background and credit history reports. **CrimSAFE** is the automated criminal background screening product involved in the Carmen Arroyo case.
- **Global footprint**: Operations in US, Canada, UK, Australia, New Zealand, India, Germany, Brazil.
- **Named one of Top 25 Global Companies in the 2024 IDC FinTech Rankings Program.**

### 2. RealPage

- **Ownership**: Acquired by **Thoma Bravo** for **$10.2 billion** (all-cash) in April 2021. Stockholder vote March 8, 2021.
- **Pre-acquisition revenue**: **$1.2 billion** (FY2020), up 17% YoY. FY2019: $980 million. FY2018: $860 million.
- **Scale**: Services used to manage **24+ million housing units** worldwide (multifamily, commercial, single-family, vacation rentals).
- **DOJ Antitrust Action (2024-present)**:
  - August 2024: DOJ + 8 Attorneys General filed antitrust lawsuit alleging RealPage's pricing algorithm enabled "systematic coordination of rental housing prices" violating century-old antitrust law.
  - January 7, 2025: DOJ added 2 more AGs and sued **6 of the largest U.S. landlords** (including Greystar and Cortland) for participating in the rent-fixing scheme.
  - April 2025: New Jersey AG Matt Platkin filed separate lawsuit against RealPage and 10 NJ landlords, calling them "a rent-setting cartel."
  - **Greystar and Cortland** reached cooperation settlements with DOJ.
  - RealPage abruptly replaced its CEO as lawsuits advanced.
- **YieldStar / AI Revenue Management**: Rebranded from YieldStar to "AI Revenue Management." ProPublica exposed in October 2022 that landlords used the algorithm to systematically increase rents, sometimes intentionally leaving units vacant to drive up demand. The company's website was reportedly scrubbed of former marketing language.
- **Marketing language (historical)**: "The industry's only price optimization solution powered by next-generation data that makes it possible to consistently reduce vacancies and maximize rents." Current: "AI-driven algorithms optimize revenue by providing precise recommendations for new and renewal leases" and "reducing turnover while optimizing the rent roll." Also claimed algorithm "helps eliminate the risk of collusion that could occur with manual pricing" -- a claim effectively inverted by the DOJ antitrust suit.
- **FTC enforcement**: $3 million settlement (2018) for inaccurate criminal record matching in tenant screening.

### 3. TransUnion

- **Revenue**: **$4.184 billion** (FY2024), a 9% increase over 2023.
- **Net income**: $284 million (FY2024), compared to -$206 million in 2023.
- **Adjusted net income**: $769 million (FY2024).
- **Tenant screening division**: TransUnion Rental Screening Solutions, Inc. (TURSS) operates the **SmartMove** product.
- **SmartMove**: Marketed directly to individual landlords. Products include **ResidentScore** (350-850 range), credit reports, criminal background checks, eviction history, and "Income Insights."
- **Marketing claims**: "Predicts evictions 15% more often than a typical credit score" and "identifies 19% more skips."
- **Enforcement**: $15 million FTC/CFPB settlement (2023) for fabricating evictions (double-counting events, labeling disputed amounts as "Judgment Amount," including sealed/expunged records).

### 4. LexisNexis Risk Solutions (RELX Group)

- **Parent company**: RELX (formerly Reed Elsevier), publicly traded (London/Amsterdam/NYSE).
- **RELX total revenue**: **GBP 9,434 million** (~$12 billion USD) in 2024.
- **Risk Solutions segment**: ~35% of RELX revenue = approximately **GBP 3.3 billion** (~$4.2 billion USD) in 2024. Revenue grew 8% in 2024 driven by AI-enabled tools.
- **Data scale**:
  - **83 billion public records** on **282 million unique identities** (~290 records per person)
  - **6+ petabytes** of data
  - More than 20 years of referential data from ~3 billion consumer records
  - Digital Identity Network: analyzes **100+ million events per day**, contains **1.4 billion unique online identities**
  - Uses "billions of digital and physical data points" for identity verification
- **CLUE Database (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange)**:
  - Contains **270+ million claims events**
  - **99.65% industry contribution** from insurers
  - Covers up to **7 years** of auto and home/property insurance claims
  - Acquired via the **ChoicePoint acquisition** (February 2008, **$3.6 billion** cash deal by Reed Elsevier)
- **Key products**: CLUE Auto, CLUE Property, Risk Classifier (200-997 score), InstantID, FraudPoint, Telematics OnDemand.

### 5. HireVue

- **Revenue**: **$89-100 million** (2024 estimates vary by source).
- **Customers**: **25,000 customers** including over **60% of the Fortune 100**.
- **Scale**: Hosted **70+ million video interviews** and **200+ million chat-based candidate engagements** to date.
- **Funding**: Raised **$93.4 million** across 8 funding rounds from 14 investors.
- **Ownership**: Acquired by **The Carlyle Group** in September 2019. Other investors include Sequoia Capital, TCV, HighGear Ventures, Frazier VC.
- **Pricing**: Starts at ~$35,000/year, typically exceeds $50,000 for enterprise contracts.
- **Controversy**: Dropped facial analysis January 2021 after audit showed it contributed "about 0.25% to a model's predictive power." Subject of EPIC FTC complaint (2019), BIPA class action in Illinois (2022), ACLU/EEOC charges (March 2025).

### 6. Verisk Analytics

- **Revenue**: **$2.882 billion** (FY2024), 7.5% increase from 2023. Q4 2024: $736 million.
- **Insurance segment**: ~$1.9 billion (2024), the dominant revenue contributor.
- **Data scale**: **32+ billion standardized insurance transaction records** -- described as "operationally and financially difficult to replicate."
- **DrivingDNA Score**: 260 billion miles of driving behavior from 8+ million telematics-equipped vehicles (Ford, GM, Honda, Hyundai). Claims "12 times difference in expected losses between worst- and best-scoring groups."
- **Business model**: Subscriptions = **80%+ of revenue** (2024). Publicly traded: NYSE: VRSK.

### 7. AppFolio

- **Revenue**: **$794.2 million** (FY2024), **28% YoY growth**. Growth driven primarily by tenant screening, electronic payments, and risk mitigation services.
- **Scale**: 20,784 property management customers managing **8.7 million units** (6% increase YoY).
- **Tenant screening**: Integrated screening is a core revenue driver; specific screening revenue not broken out but identified as a primary growth contributor in earnings reports.
- **Publicly traded**: NASDAQ: APPF.

### 8. Yardi Systems

- **Revenue**: ~**$1.1 billion** annual revenue (estimated; privately held).
- **Market share**: ~**10.6%** in real estate and property management software.
- **Tenant screening**: Enhanced by acquisition of **RentGrow**, a resident screening platform.
- **Competitors**: AppFolio, RealPage, Buildium.
- **Privately held**: Founded 1984, Santa Barbara, CA.

### 9. Sift (formerly Sift Science)

- **Revenue**: ~**$30.3 million** (October 2024).
- **Valuation**: **$1 billion+** (April 2021 funding round; raised $50M at that valuation).
- **Customers**: 700+ enterprise customers; 34,000+ sites and apps.
- **How it works**: Real-time scoring based on **16,000+ signals** using cloud-based ML. Analyzes fraud patterns including page navigation sequences, IP ranges, email patterns, graph connectivity, browser configs.

### 10. Palantir Technologies

- **Consumer scoring**: Does not market traditional consumer scoring products. However:
  - 2014: Launched **Insightics** -- extracts customer spending/demographic info from merchants' credit card records (per WSJ).
  - **Gotham platform** integrates consumer data: web browsing history, social network interactions, online behavior.
  - Patents describe integrating "consumer entity data systems" across multiple data sources.
- **Law enforcement/immigration**:
  - Used by LAPD, NYPD, New Orleans Police, ICE, regional fusion centers.
  - ICE has spent **$200+ million** on Palantir contracts for Investigative Case Management.
  - April 2025: ICE signed **$30 million** contract for near real-time migrant tracking -- aggregating border entries, visa records, home addresses, social media activity.
  - Gotham integrates: DMV records, license plate readers (ALPR), credit databases, government benefit databases, biometric data, social media.
  - JPMorgan Chase was first commercial client (2009) for fraud detection.
- **Revenue**: **$2.87 billion** (FY2024). NYSE: PLTR. Now serves 40+ industries.
- **Position**: "Palantir is not a surveillance company" -- while Gotham is widely used for surveillance-adjacent functions by law enforcement and immigration agencies.

### 11. Acxiom / LiveRamp

- **Corporate history**: In 2018, Acxiom sold its Marketing Services division to **Interpublic Group**. Remaining company became **LiveRamp Holdings** (NYSE: RAMP).
- **LiveRamp revenue**: **$659.7 million** (FY2024, ending March 31, 2024), 10.6% increase from 2023.
- **Data scale**: Consumer data on **260 million individuals** across **162+ million U.S. households** -- demographics, purchase behavior, interests, financial indicators, health data segments.
- **Identity graph**: RampID connects online/offline identifiers across channels. Ecosystem of 500+ partners.
- **Customers**: 900 direct subscription customers; 115 with contracts exceeding $1M/year.
- **Acxiom (under Interpublic)**: Up to **3,000 data points per person** on hundreds of millions of consumers. One of the largest data brokers globally.

## MARKET SIZES

### Tenant Screening Market
- **2024 estimate**: **$1.5-2.0 billion** (housing advocates: $1.3B+; Credence Research: $1.5B in 2023; Dataintelo: $1.95B in 2024)
- **Projected**: $3.7-5.8 billion by 2032-2033 (CAGR 7.2-9.3%)
- ~2,000 companies offer screening software; **9 in 10 landlords** use screening reports
- Shelterforce: "a billion-dollar industry with little oversight"

### Employment Screening Market
- **2024 estimate**: **$4.9-6.5 billion** (narrower employment screening); **$14.7-15.5 billion** (broader background check market)
- **Projected**: Employment screening to $10.5-19.6B by 2033-2035 (CAGR 6.5-11.2%); background checks to $25.9-39.6B by 2030-2032
- **82% of companies** now use AI to review resumes (ResumeBuilder)

### Insurance Scoring / Analytics Market
- **Verisk alone**: $2.88B (2024); **LexisNexis Risk**: ~$4.2B (2024)
- **Combined Verisk + LexisNexis Risk**: ~$7 billion annually from insurance-related analytics
- Broader insurance analytics market (scoring, claims, underwriting tools): estimated **$10-15 billion** globally
- Credit-based insurance scores used by nearly all major insurers; banned/restricted in CA, HI, MA, MD

### Data Broker Market
- Major players: Acxiom/LiveRamp, Experian, CoreLogic/Cotality, Equifax, Oracle, Nielsen, LexisNexis
- LiveRamp alone: $660M revenue (FY2024)

## PRIVATE EQUITY OWNERSHIP OF SCREENING COMPANIES

**The concentration of scoring infrastructure in private equity hands is a critical underreported story.**

### Thoma Bravo
- **AUM**: $183+ billion (2026)
- **Portfolio**: 77+ software companies, ~$30B annual revenue, 93,000+ employees
- **Strategy**: "Buy and build" -- acquires software companies, takes them private (removing public financial disclosure), optimizes for revenue
- **Key acquisitions**:
  - **RealPage**: $10.2B (April 2021) -- tenant screening/rent pricing for 24M+ housing units
  - **SailPoint**: $6.9B (April 2022) -- identity governance
  - **Ping Identity**: $2.8B (August 2022) -- identity security
  - **ForgeRock**: $2.3B (October 2022) -- identity management (merged with Ping, August 2023)
  - Total identity platform: ~$12B across SailPoint, Ping, ForgeRock
- **565+ total software transactions** completed
- **Significance**: Controls both tenant screening infrastructure (RealPage, 24M+ units) and major identity platforms -- enormous power over who gets verified/scored/granted access, shielded from public market transparency

### The Carlyle Group
- **AUM**: ~$426 billion (2024)
- **Key acquisition**: **HireVue** (September 2019) -- 70M+ interviews, 25K customers, 60% of Fortune 100
- After Carlyle acquisition, HireVue continued expanding AI tools while facing mounting regulatory scrutiny

### Stone Point Capital & Insight Partners
- **Key acquisition**: **CoreLogic** (now Cotality) for **$5.9 billion** (February 2021)
- Took CoreLogic private; rebranded to Cotality March 2025

### The PE Playbook
1. **Acquire** company with deep consumer data assets and scoring infrastructure
2. **Take private** -- eliminate SEC reporting and public scrutiny
3. **Optimize for revenue** -- expand data collection and licensing
4. **Consolidate** -- merge with adjacent companies to create data moats
5. **Shield from accountability** -- private companies face less regulatory/media/investor pressure

## DATA PARTNERSHIPS -- WHERE SCREENING COMPANIES GET THEIR DATA

### Court Records
- **Eviction records**: Scraped from court websites immediately upon filing. Even dismissed/sealed cases persist in databases.
- **Criminal records**: From electronic court records, sex offender registries, watch lists. **87% of NY criminal records contain errors**; **69% missing outcome data**.
- **Matching problem**: No SSNs in court records. RealPage matched "Anthony Jones" to "Antoinette Jones."

### Credit Bureaus
- TransUnion, Experian, Equifax provide credit data to screening companies
- TransUnion both operates SmartMove and wholesales data to third-party screeners

### Public Records Aggregators
- LexisNexis: 83B records from public records and proprietary sources
- CoreLogic/Cotality: Property records, real estate transactions, ownership data
- Sources: DMV, property tax, voter registration, professional licenses, UCC filings, bankruptcy, liens/judgments

### Data Brokers
- **Acxiom/LiveRamp**: 260M individuals, 162M+ households, 3,000 data points/person
- **LexisNexis**: Billions of records from thousands of sources; 1.4B online identities
- Data flows between brokers -- same consumer data appears in multiple scoring systems

### Proprietary / Behavioral Data
- **Verisk**: 260B miles of driving data (Ford, GM, Honda, Hyundai); 32B+ insurance records
- **RealPage**: 30M+ lease outcomes for ML training
- **HireVue**: 70M+ video interviews, 200M+ chat interactions as training data
- **Sift**: 16,000+ fraud signals across 34,000+ sites

### The Data Supply Chain
Courts/public records -> aggregators (LexisNexis, CoreLogic) -> screening companies (RealPage, SafeRent, TransUnion) -> landlords/employers/insurers -> instant approve/deny. Errors compound at each step. Data collected for one purpose (dismissed court filing) gets repurposed (denying housing years later). Consumers have no visibility into most transfers.

## MARKETING LANGUAGE -- HOW THEY SELL IT

### Direct Quotes / Near-Quotes from Company Materials

- **RealPage**: "The first AI-based screening algorithm built specifically for the multifamily apartment rental industry." Historical: "The industry's only price optimization solution powered by next-generation data that makes it possible to consistently reduce vacancies and maximize rents." Also claimed algorithm "helps eliminate the risk of collusion that could occur with manual pricing" -- a claim effectively inverted by the DOJ antitrust suit. Current: "AI-driven algorithms optimize revenue by providing precise recommendations for new and renewal leases" and "reducing turnover while optimizing the rent roll."
- **TransUnion SmartMove**: "Streamline the way you evaluate renters." "Make confident, informed decisions." "Say goodbye to manual tasks."
- **SafeRent**: "Mathematical analysis of information found in tenant screening reports"
- **Upstart**: "Approve 44% more creditworthy borrowers than FICO models, cutting rates by 36%"
- **Verisk**: "12 times difference in expected losses between worst- and best-scoring groups"
- **Eightfold**: "Superior fairness and accuracy in matching people to roles" (despite class action alleging 1B+ scraped profiles)
- **Experian** (tenant screening): Tools that "improve fraud detection, reduce costs, and streamline tenant screening"
- **Cotality** (formerly CoreLogic): "Intelligence beyond bounds" (new 2025 tagline)

### The Industry-Wide Language Pattern
"Reduce risk," "streamline decisions," "confident, informed decisions," "eliminate the risk of collusion," "smarter decisions for your rentals," "goodbye to manual tasks." The framing is always: **algorithmic = objective, human = biased/slow/expensive**. This inverts the actual evidence.
