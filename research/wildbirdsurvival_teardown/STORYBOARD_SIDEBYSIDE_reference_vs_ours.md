## THE GRAMMAR (the correlation you asked for)
Editing is not "cut every 3.6s." It's: **the story is in an emotional state → the craft matches that
state.** Every choice in the whole video is one of these seven states:

| When the story is… | Cut speed | Shot size | VO | Music | SFX | WHY (the mechanism) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ASSAULT / shock** | fast (1.4–2.2s) | FACE then MACRO | names the threat, terse | almost none | visceral (fly buzz, blood) | rapid cuts = chaos, no rest; macro = you can't look away; music silent so the gross image + word "blood" hit clean |
| **ATTRITION / exhaustion** | slow (6–12s holds) | macro + long body holds | "it has tried everything…" | sad minor pad **enters** | the physical struggle (scrape, squelch) | long holds make time *drag* so you FEEL the futility; a fast cut here would kill the weariness |
| **ANTICIPATION (pre-turn)** | freeze on ONE shot | held | **SILENCE (VO drops out)** | **swells into the silence** | drop to near-nothing | removing the voice makes a vacuum → the viewer leans in; the music carries what the words don't; this is the #1 retention device |
| **THE TURN / new act** | one clean cut | environment CHANGES | one signpost word ("Finally…") | lifts, hopeful | new-world ambience | a visual surprise (new place) signals "everything changes now" |
| **MARVEL / payoff** | STOP cutting (30–40s hold) | the spectacle, wide-ish + inserts | near-WORDLESS (≤1 line) | **PEAKS here** (first third) | the event's own sound | you came to see THIS — let it breathe; words break the spell; the emotional high lands early so they stay |
| **EXPOSITION / teach** | steady body pace (~4s) + a few 10s | variety, no two alike | denser but under the ceiling | eases into a warm middle | light ambience | the peak is past; now it's "why" — comfortable, informative, not rushed |
| **RESOLUTION / release** | slow (8–14s holds) | golden-hour WIDE | one philosophical line | **falls**, resolves, fades | wind, calm | warm light = peace; wide = restored to its world; slow = exhale; leave them settled |

Everything below is just this table applied, minute by minute.

## MOMENT BY MOMENT — THE WHOLE VIDEO

### M1 · 0:00–0:03 · THE STRIKE — "stop scrolling"
**THEM:** Shot A = **buffalo FACE, 1.6s** (a fly at the eye). Cut to Shot B = **EXTREME MACRO of engorged
ticks, 2.2s**. VO: *"Across the African savannah, this Cape buffalo is."* Music: a low sub-drone, barely
audible. SFX: **heavy fly buzz.**
**WHY:** Face first = "this is a real individual in trouble" (empathy in 1.6s). Then the gross macro by
2 seconds = the physical shock that kills the scroll — you flinch. Music stays out of the way so the buzz
and the word land. Fast because an assault should feel like one.
**OURS:** Shot A = **booby FACE/eye, 1.6s**, a fly crawls. Shot B = **EXTREME MACRO, 2.2s** — a black
finch's beak in the white down, a bead of red welling. VO: *"Right now, on a bare rock in the Pacific,"* →
*"this seabird is being drained—"*. Music: low sub-drone. SFX: wind + the tiny dry *tap* of the beak, then
a wet note as blood beads.
**WHY (ours):** identical job — the red-on-white bead is our "engorged tick," the shock that stops the scroll.

### M2 · 0:03–0:15 · THE ASSAULT — "it's besieged"
**THEM:** **BRIEF wide, 1.6s** (buffalo in the savanna — fills the frame, not a dot) → **head-shake, 1.4s**,
droplets flying. Fast cuts continue. VO: *"enduring an assault from hundreds of blood-sucking ticks."*
Music: barely there. SFX: buzz + a wet head-shake.
**WHY:** The wide is ONE quick breath for context — 1.6s so it doesn't kill momentum (their flops sat on
15s of scenery; that's the trap). The head-shake = "fighting back, losing." VO names the stakes NOW.
**OURS:** **BRIEF wide, 1.6s** — booby low on the lava, finches around it (booby clear, not tiny) →
**bill-snap, 1.4s**, finches flare. VO: *"…one drop at a time. Not by a shark. Not by a storm."*
**WHY (ours):** the snap is our "head-shake"; the wide is context, not a landscape beauty-shot.

### M3 · 0:15–0:37 · THE ATTRITION — "make me feel the exhaustion"
**THEM:** The cuts **LENGTHEN** — MACRO hold **5.8s**, MACRO hold **7.1s**, then a **12-second hold** of the
buffalo kneeling and rubbing in the dust. VO: *"It has tried everything — rubbing against tree trunks,
rolling in the dust…"* B-roll **literally matches** the words. Music: a slow **minor pad enters** — the
first real emotion. SFX: bark-scrape, dust, a low grunt.
**WHY:** This is the turn from acute→grinding. Holds get LONG so time drags and you feel the weariness. A
quick cut here would undercut it. Literal b-roll makes the narration feel true. The minor music arriving now
tells you "this is sad," before any predator appears.
**OURS:** MACRO hold **5.8s** (finch drinking) → hold **7.1s** (a second finch at the tail-base) → **12s
hold**: booby shaking, snapping, twisting; the finches simply hop back on. VO: *"It has tried everything —
shaking them off, snapping, twisting away. They just hop back on."* Music: minor pad enters. SFX: wind,
finch chatter, a low booby groan.
**WHY (ours):** the 12s hold of futile struggle is the emotional core of the setup — the point where the
viewer thinks "it can't win."

### M4 · 0:37–0:48 · BREAKING DOWN — the wordless jolt + the toll
**THEM:** **Motion beat, 1.8s, WORDLESS** — buffalo bucking near a tree (a jolt of energy in the slow
stretch) → **9.5s hold** on it rubbing a tree trunk, the raw skin. VO returns: *"against tree trunks…"*
Music: the pad holds. SFX: the physical impacts.
**WHY:** One short wordless jolt keeps the slow section from going flat, then straight back to a long hold
on the damage. The wordless beat also trains the viewer that silence = something's happening.
**OURS:** **Motion beat, 1.8s, WORDLESS** — the booby jerks hard, a finch flutters up → **9.5s hold** on the
raw, weeping wound, a fly on it. VO: *"And still, every hour, a little more of it is carried away."*
**WHY (ours):** the wordless jerk is the jolt; the hold on the wound is the "toll" — the cost made physical.

### M5 · 0:48–1:05 · THE SILENCE — the most important 15 seconds
**THEM:** **ONE held shot, ~14.9 seconds, MUSIC ONLY** — the buffalo collapsed, exhausted, then rising and
walking toward the river. **No narration at all.** Music **swells** into the gap. SFX: near-nothing.
**WHY:** This is the breath before the reveal. Cutting the voice for 15s creates a vacuum the viewer
leans into — "what now?" The swell carries the emotion the words aren't saying. **This single choice — a
long silent pause before the turn — is the biggest retention lever in the whole video.**
**OURS:** **ONE held shot, ~14s, MUSIC ONLY** — the booby stills, eye half-closing, then a slow push-in on
the finch sitting on its back: a plain, blunt **seed-cracking beak.** No VO. Music swells.
**WHY (ours):** the silence sets up our reveal (that this is an ordinary seed-eater) exactly like their
pre-river pause sets up the fish.

### M6 · 1:05–1:35 · THE TURN — a new world
**THEM:** One clean cut as the buffalo enters the river; the camera goes **UNDERWATER** — a totally new
visual palette (green, weightless). VO returns with the signpost: *"Finally…"* Music lifts, hopeful.
**WHY:** A hard change of *environment* announces a new act — the eye registers "everything is different
now" before the words say it.
**OURS:** The reveal cut: the same finch beak, now **red-tipped.** VO: *"This is a ground finch. A
seed-eater. It has no business wanting blood at all — so how did it learn? And why does a seabird twenty
times its size just let it happen?"* Music lifts into a low, curious motif.
**WHY (ours):** our "new world" is a new *understanding* (the double curiosity-gap question) rather than a
new location — the reveal reframes everything you just watched.

### M7 · 1:35–3:00 · THE MARVEL — stop cutting, let them stare (**music peaks ~2:19**)
**THEM:** A **single held take ~34.6 seconds** of hundreds of fish pressing their mouths to the buffalo,
cleaning it — **almost no narration** over it. Then a few inserts. Music **PEAKS here** — the fullest,
warmest it gets in the whole video. SFX: underwater ambience, bubbles.
**WHY:** This is the payoff you clicked for. You STOP cutting so the viewer can just *watch the spectacle*
— words would break the spell, so density drops near zero and the image carries it. The music peaks in
the **first third** (not the middle/end) so the dopamine hit lands early and holds them for the back half.
Peaking dead-center is what their FLOP did.
**OURS:** A **~30–40 s held take** of the finch drinking at the wound — throat working, the booby's eye
softening, the other finches busy around it. **≤1 sentence over it:** *"It drinks. And for the first time
in days, the seabird goes still."* Music opens up, warm-but-dark. SFX: tiny beak-taps, a long booby exhale.
**WHY (ours):** the drink-hold IS our money shot — the gross-lane spectacle held long and near-wordless;
music peaks here, first third, same as theirs.

### M8 · 3:00–4:30 · THE WHY IT HAPPENS — exposition, back to body pace
**THEM:** Cuts return to **~4s body pace**, lots of shot VARIETY (fish feeding, the calm buffalo, close and
wide), a couple of 10s holds on wonder-beats. VO is **denser now** (explaining the mutualism) but stays
under the coverage ceiling. Music **eases down** from the peak into a warm, contented middle. SFX: light.
**WHY:** The emotional high is spent; now the brain wants "why." Steady 4s cuts feel informative and
comfortable — not rushed, not draggy. Variety keeps it alive while the VO does the teaching.
**OURS:** ~4s cuts, variety: the **old mutualism** (finch picking mites off the booby) ×3 → the **pivot**
(a beak breaks skin, a bead, the finch drinks) → intercut clean-vs-bloodied. VO: *"It didn't begin as
cruelty. These finches earned their place picking off the ticks and flies the big bird couldn't reach. A
fair trade — until a beak drew blood. And a bird starving on a bare rock doesn't forget the taste."*
Music eases to a steady, darkening middle.
**WHY (ours):** the "bargain that soured" is our mutualism explainer — same job, darker turn.

### M9 · 4:30–5:15 · THE PLACE THAT MADE IT — widen the lens
**THEM:** A few establishing **wides (buffalo clear, brief)** + the ecosystem — situating the relationship
in its world. Body pace, some holds. VO continues the exposition. Music wanders, a touch of wonder.
**WHY:** After the intimate payoff, one pull-back to "here's the bigger picture" gives scale and a breather
before the back half — but the wides are still brief and the animal is always the subject.
**OURS:** The barren truth of Wolf & Darwin — black lava, no water, prickly-pear, the sea on all sides,
drought, dusk. Finch drinks nectar → pecks guano → returns to the booby. VO: *"These are the two most
remote islands in the Galápagos. No streams, no soft ground. Half the year the rain stops and the seeds
run out — so the finch drinks whatever it can. Flower nectar. Seabird droppings. And, when there's nothing
else, the one thing that never runs dry on an island full of birds."* **[PAUSE]** *"Blood."*
**WHY (ours):** widen to the island = the environment that FORCED the behavior; the held word "Blood."
after a pause is a mini-reveal.

### M10 · 5:15–7:10 · THE SECOND JEOPARDY (our differentiator) — build, then the wordless burst
**THEM:** buffalo/fish is single-arc, so here they *deepen* the relationship (life together) at body pace;
their **combat** videos (855K) instead put a fight here. We take the combat lane.
**OURS — build 5:15–6:15:** cuts **tighten** (5→2s), tension pulse enters. Tilt down to reveal the **egg**
under the booby; a finch eyes it; the booby pins it. VO: *"There's a deeper reason it won't leave. Beneath
it is an egg — and these same finches are egg-thieves. The moment it lifts off to escape them, they take
what it was protecting. So it sits still, and bleeds."* Music curdles into a low tension drone + a ticking
pulse.
**OURS — the raid 6:15–7:10:** **10 sub-2s cuts, WORDLESS, ~40s of music+SFX only** — wing lifts, finches
rush the exposed skin, an egg rolled an inch, the bill stabs down, dust, the egg rocked back under, finches
scatter and re-land. VO: **none.** Music: peak intensity, then snaps to near-silence as the raid breaks off.
**WHY (ours):** the wordless fast-cut burst is the action climax — the same "let the event run wordless"
principle as their money shot, but at maximum energy instead of maximum calm.

### M11 · 7:10–8:00 · THE RESOLUTION — release (**music falls**)
**THEM:** **Golden-hour WIDE**, the buffalo (now clean) walking away; **slow 8–14s holds**; one philosophical
theme line; a departing shot. Music **falls and resolves**, fades under wind. **No "subscribe" in the VO**
(it lives in the description).
**WHY:** Warm light = emotional peace; the wide returns the animal to its world; slow holds let the viewer
exhale; the theme line gives the "meaning" they'll remember. End cinematic, not salesy.
**OURS:** **Grey-dawn wide** (our tone isn't golden — it's bleak-beautiful): finches resettle on the booby's
back; it resettles over its egg, flank streaked with dried blood; final wide of the lone booby walking into
the mist, birds riding. Slow 8–14s holds. VO: *"By morning it's quiet again. The seabird keeps its egg. The
finch keeps its life. Out here, at the edge of the world, survival isn't kindness — and it isn't cruelty. It's
only the bargain the island allows, paid one drop of blood at a time."* Music falls, gentle, fades under wind.
**WHY (ours):** same release, our palette — the theme line is the "meaning" beat.
