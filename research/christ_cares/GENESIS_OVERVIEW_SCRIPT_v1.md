## SCRIPT

**[S01 — black; a voice in the darkness; then light floods a formless ocean. God = brilliant light above the waters, no form]**
"In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." Ten words — and they answer the biggest question ever asked: where did everything come from? Not chance. Not chaos. God.

**[S02 — six-day montage: waters divide, land rises, sun ignites, seas swarm, land animals emerge — great dragon-like creatures among the day-six beasts]**
In six days, God speaks the universe into order. Light and time. Sky and sea. Land bursting green. Stars flung across the dark. Seas that suddenly swarm. Then the land animals — every kind, mighty and small. And yes — that means the great creatures we dig up as bones today walked this same young earth. We'll get to them.

**[S03 — a garden; a man formed from dust by hands of light; then the woman; both figures rim-lit, faces unseen]**
Then God does something different. He doesn't just speak — He forms a man from the dust and breathes life into him. Adam. Then Eve. Made in the image of God Himself. And God's own verdict over it all: "indeed it was very good."

**[S04 — the two trees; the serpent coiled; the fruit taken; shadows lengthen across the garden]**
One tree is off limits. One. Not because God was hiding something — but because love that cannot choose is not love. Then a serpent whispers the first lie ever told on earth: "Has God indeed said...?" They eat. And everything breaks. Shame. Hiding. Blame. Death enters the world — exactly as God warned.

**[S05 — judgment scene; a single shaft of light cuts the darkness]**
But listen to what God says to the serpent — because this is the most important sentence in the book: "He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel." One day a Son, born of woman, will crush the serpent — and be wounded doing it. Genesis 3:15 is the Bible's first promise of Christ. The rest of the Bible is that promise coming true.

**[S06 — a field; blood on the ground; cities rising; violence spreading in quick cuts]**
Outside the garden, sin grows up fast. The first child ever born murders the second. And generation by generation, violence fills the earth, until every thought of man's heart is only evil, continually.

**[S07 — the ark towering; rain; the fountains of the deep erupting; a global ocean; a door closed by light]**
Judgment comes as a worldwide flood — and grace comes as a boat. "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD." One family. One door. God Himself shuts them in. The waters cover the highest mountains — and when they finally recede, God hangs His bow in the clouds. Never again.

**[S08 — Babel rising; the scattering; a map tightening onto one man in Ur]**
Man unites again — not to honor God, but to make a name for himself. A tower. So God scatters them into languages and nations. And then the account narrows... all the way down... to one man.

**[S09 — Abram walking out of a city gate toward open desert and stars]**
God calls Abram: leave everything, and go to a land I will show you. And He makes a staggering promise: "In you all the families of the earth shall be blessed." All of them. Every nation on earth — through one family. The family the Serpent-Crusher will come from.

**[S10 — an ocean of stars; an old man looking up]**
Abram is old. His wife is barren. It is impossible. That's the point. "And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness." Not earned — believed. That is how anyone has ever been made right with God, on every page since.

**[S11 — a ram caught in a thicket on the mountain; a man wrestling till dawn, silhouetted]**
The promise passes to Isaac — spared on a mountain when God provides the sacrifice Himself. Then to Jacob — the schemer who wrestles with God through the night and limps away with a new name: Israel.

**[S12 — the coat; the pit; chains; then the throne room of Egypt]**
And then Joseph. Sold by his own brothers. Enslaved. Slandered. Imprisoned. Forgotten. Then raised, in a single day, to the right hand of Pharaoh — to save the very brothers who betrayed him. His verdict over his own life is the verdict over the whole book: "You meant evil against me; but God meant it for good."

**[S13 — a deathbed blessing; one son bathed in light; a scepter of light held out over the earth]**
Genesis closes with one more promise: the scepter will not depart from Judah. A King is coming through this family. Genesis opens with God creating everything — and closes with God quietly steering everything. Fifty chapters. Not fairy tales. The true account of where you came from, what went wrong — and the promise God made to fix it.

**[S14 — CTA card, faceless light motif]**
Next: the six days of creation, one chapter at a time — including the questions everyone asks. The dinosaurs. The flood. Cain's wife. The Nephilim. Subscribe — and tonight, read Genesis 1 for yourself. The original is better than any retelling. Including this one.
