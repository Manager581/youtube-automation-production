## HOW TO READ THE TABLE
- **Horizon %** = measured height of the strongest horizontal luma break, as % from frame top. **Low number = camera is HIGH looking down. High number = camera is LOW looking up.** "—" = no clean horizon (underwater/black).
- **Subj %** = % of frame occupied by non-background pixels (rough subject size).
- **Bright / Sat** = mean luma (0–255) and mean saturation %.
- **Mo** = mean absolute luma change vs the previous second. **>30 = a cut.**

## WHAT THE MEASUREMENTS REVEAL

**1. Camera height is the main storytelling tool — and it alternates hard.**
The horizon never sits near the middle. It is either **6–22%** (camera high, looking down — the animal is small, observed, vulnerable) or **78–98%** (camera low, looking up — the animal is huge, dominant, close). He flips between these two poles at almost every cut. Eye-level is used exactly once: **the hero portrait at 24–26 s.** That is why it lands.

**2. Subject size collapses at the turn.** The Spino occupies **69% of frame at the hero shot (26 s)** and **23% at 47 s** while being dragged down. The power transfer is expressed as *framing*, not action.

**3. Saturation is a three-act arc and it flips exactly on the act breaks.**
17% (swamp) → **77% peak (open water, 36 s)** → 19% (the kill, 49 s). The saturation drop at second 49 is the single most violent change in the film — the colour dies at the same instant the animal does.

**4. There is no camera movement anywhere.** Every shot is locked off or a slow drift. Within-shot motion runs 6–24; anything over 30 is a cut. No pans, no pushes, no handheld, no speed ramps.

**5. The quietest, stillest moment is immediately before the threat.**
Seconds 35–37: motion bottoms out at **6.6**, saturation peaks at **77%** — jellyfish drifting, nothing happening. The dark shape arrives at 41. He buys the shock with 6 seconds of calm.

**6. The kill gets 27% of the runtime — and it is the ugliest footage in the film.**
Seconds 49–65 are the most detail-dense, lowest-dead-space, most degraded shots. They also get the longest holds. This is the film's weakest trade and the thing NOT to copy.

**7. Every violent frame is dark and obscured.** Contact frames average **25% darker** than clean single-subject frames (mean luma 64 vs 85). Blood, foam, silt and motion blur cover every point of contact — because the generator cannot render coherent two-creature contact. **The failure is disguised as style.**
