# Research Brief: Why Breaking the Law Is Profitable

## Title
Why Breaking the Law Is Profitable

## Thesis
For the biggest companies in the world, breaking the law isn't a risk — it's a LINE ITEM.
They budget for it. They calculate the fine vs the profit and choose to break the law every time.
Corporate penalties in the US have surpassed $1 TRILLION since 2000. The companies paid it.
And kept going. Because the math always works in their favor.

## Killer Stats (verified)
1. **$1 TRILLION** — total corporate fines/penalties in the US since 2000 (Good Jobs First)
2. **$0** — RealPage's fine for coordinating rent across 10M+ apartments (DOJ, March 2026)
3. **$5 BILLION** — Meta's FTC fine. Stock went UP the day it was announced. = 9% of one year's revenue
4. **16 days** — Boeing's $2.5B settlement as proportion of annual revenue ($58.2B in 2020). 346 people died.
5. **$1.5 BILLION** — Anthropic's settlement for downloading pirated books to train Claude (Sep 2025)
6. **90 lawsuits** — active copyright cases against AI companies as of March 2026
7. **127 companies** — have each paid more than $1B in fines since 2000
8. **Recidivist companies get SMALLER fines** as a percentage of revenue than first-time offenders

## Structure (6 Acts, ~35 minutes)

### ACT 1: THE MATH (0:00-6:00)
Cold open with the $0 RealPage headline from this week.
"A company that coordinates rent prices for 10 million apartments just settled with the DOJ.
The penalty? Nothing. No fine. No admission of guilt. Renters got nothing."
Then: "This isn't a bug. It's the business model."
Introduce the thesis: corporate fines are a line item, not a punishment.
$1 trillion in fines since 2000. Companies paid it all. And they're still here. Still profitable.
The math: average corporate fine is less than the profit from the violation.

**Case Study: META/FACEBOOK**
- Violated 2012 FTC consent decree by selling user data
- FTC fine: $5 billion — sounds massive
- Reality: 9% of ONE year's revenue
- Stock price went UP the day the fine was announced
- Investors literally cheered — the uncertainty was priced in, the fine was a relief
- Facebook's message to the market: "We can break the law for $5 billion. That's a bargain."

### ACT 2: THE PLAYBOOK (6:00-13:00)
How this became standard corporate strategy.

**Case Study: BOEING**
- 737 MAX: software flaw known internally, hidden from FAA
- 346 people died in two crashes (Lion Air 610, Ethiopian Airlines 302)
- Settlement: $2.5 billion = 16 days of Boeing's 2020 revenue
- Breakdown: $243.6M criminal penalty, $1.77B to airlines (not victims), $500M victim fund
- NO executive went to prison
- Boeing later VIOLATED the settlement terms — DOJ found non-compliance
- Cost of compliance (fixing the software properly): estimated $500M-1B
- Cost of non-compliance (fines + settlements): $2.5B but spread over years
- The math was close enough that Boeing chose to ship the flawed plane

**Case Study: WELLS FARGO**
- Created 3.5 million fake accounts without customer knowledge
- Total fines: ~$3 billion
- Wells Fargo annual revenue: ~$80 billion
- Fine = ~2 weeks of revenue
- 5,300 employees fired. CEO retired with full benefits.
- Wells Fargo is still one of the largest banks in America

**Pattern: Fine < Profit = Do It**
127 companies have each paid over $1 billion in fines since 2000.
The most penalized industries: financial services and pharmaceuticals.
These are also the most PROFITABLE industries.
The fines aren't stopping them — they're the price of admission.

### ACT 3: THE AI WARS (13:00-22:00)
THIS IS HAPPENING RIGHT NOW. The biggest data heist in history.

**The Strategy: Scrape Now, Pay Later, Keep The Model**
Every major AI company scraped the entire internet without permission.
The training data is already baked into the models. You can't un-train it.
Even if they lose every lawsuit, they keep the AI. The fine will always be less than the model's value.

**Case Study: ANTHROPIC** (our most current example)
- Downloaded millions of pirated books from shadow libraries to train Claude
- Settled for $1.5 billion (September 2025) — largest AI copyright settlement ever
- Anthropic's valuation: $60+ billion
- Settlement = ~2.5% of company value
- The model trained on those books is STILL running. Every Claude conversation uses that data.
- Universal Music + others filed ANOTHER $3.1 billion suit in January 2026

**Case Study: OPENAI**
- NYT sued for "billions" — case ongoing
- Judge ordered OpenAI to hand over 20 million ChatGPT logs (Jan 2026)
- OpenAI's response: fight in court for years, the model is already trained
- Meanwhile, OpenAI raised another $6.6 billion in funding
- The lawsuits are a rounding error compared to the value created

**Case Study: REDDIT vs PERPLEXITY (Oct 2025)**
- Reddit caught Perplexity scraping with a trap post (indexed by Google but otherwise inaccessible)
- Perplexity's citations to Reddit INCREASED 40x AFTER receiving a cease-and-desist
- They literally scraped MORE after being told to stop
- Why? Because the data is more valuable than any fine

**90 active copyright lawsuits** against AI companies as of March 2026.
Three of five major studios have sued Midjourney.
Disney sent Google a cease-and-desist.
None of this has slowed any AI company down. Not one.

### ACT 4: THE RENT (22:00-28:00)
Back to where we started. The freshest case.

**Case Study: REALPAGE (March 2026 — THIS WEEK)**
- Software company, $1.6B annual revenue
- Owned by Thoma Bravo (private equity), bought for $10.2 billion in 2021
- Their algorithm: takes nonpublic rent data from competing landlords, recommends prices
- Effect: coordinated rent increases across 10+ million apartments nationwide
- Landlords followed 90% of the algorithm's suggestions
- DOJ filed suit August 2024, alleging Sherman Act violation (price fixing)
- Settlement (Nov 2025, finalized March 2026):
  - $0 in financial penalties
  - $0 compensation for renters
  - $0 admission of guilt
  - Must stop using competitors' nonpublic data
  - Court-appointed monitor
- Greystar (landlord co-defendant): paid California $7 million
- RealPage's estimated profit from the scheme: billions over multiple years
- The algorithm is STILL running, just with "restrictions"

### ACT 5: THE PATTERN (28:00-33:00)
Zoom out. Show the system.

**The Trillion Dollar Pattern:**
- Since 2000: $1 trillion in corporate penalties
- Annual payouts: grew from $7B/year (early 2000s) to $50B+/year (recent)
- 127 parent companies have each paid $1B+ in penalties
- The most penalized companies are REPEAT offenders
- Repeat offenders get SMALLER fines as % of revenue
- Translation: the system rewards recidivism

**Why This Keeps Happening:**
1. Fines are capped or negotiated down
2. Settlements avoid trial (no precedent set)
3. No individual executives go to prison
4. Companies can deduct some penalties as business expenses
5. The market rewards "certainty" — stock goes up when fine is announced
6. Compliance costs more than non-compliance for most violations

**The Ford Pinto Memo (historical anchor):**
Ford calculated that fixing the Pinto's exploding gas tank would cost $137M.
Estimated wrongful death payouts: $49.5M.
They chose to let people die. This was 1977.
The practice didn't end — it got more sophisticated.
