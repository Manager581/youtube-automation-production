# "I Tested if YouTube is Pay To Win" — Fabio Morena (ROPkHP8jpW0, 20:44, 136K views)

## What the video is
A case-study "experiment": two faceless tier-list channels posting the same 4 videos (same titles, same upload times, same VO — his own voice on both). Cheap channel ($270/video: AI research, AI script, AI thumbnail, $250 editor) vs expensive channel ($2,010/video: $100 researcher, $250 writer, $1,600 editor, $60 thumbnail designer). Plus a side test of YouTube's built-in Promotions feature.

## What's actually shown (evidence)
- Ideation by mashup [02:02–03:36]: celebrity car tier list 4.8M + streamer car collection 7.8M → "streamer car tier list"; billionaire house 7.7M + presidential protection 1.7M → "billionaire protection tier list."
- Cost sheets on screen [05:44–06:44]. Results [18:27–19:00]: expensive channel 1M views / 3,700 subs from 4 videos; cheap channel 10K views / 381 subs (380 of them paid → 1 organic sub).
- Paid Promotions [16:54–18:00]: $200 → 1,419 views + 380 subs ($0.14/view, $0.53/sub). His verdict: not worth it, except to cross the sub threshold for monetization when watch hours are already met.
- Late update [19:28–20:15]: cheap channel jumped 10K → 44K+ views and 450 subs weeks later, "randomly, out of nowhere," with no changes.
- Thumbnail comp for tier lists [11:49–12:51]: two big subjects + one obviously worse one in the middle + name text; his AI thumbnail (reference image + prompt) beat the $60 human one in his own judgment.

## Credibility
**Fatally confounded experiment he never addresses: the expensive channel was a rebranded AGED channel [01:01], the cheap one created fresh [02:02].** Aged-channel authority alone could explain 1M vs 10K. n=4 videos per arm, one niche, no retention data shown. The mid-roll is a Skool community pitch (skool.com/faceless, "$5K/month," "$2.6M from Binge Central" — unverifiable), and the "quality beats AI slop" conclusion conveniently sells his teaching — then his own late update (cheap channel 4.4x-ing untouched) undercuts it. The Promotions numbers are the most trustworthy artifact: small, specific, shown from the dashboard, and *against* the pay-to-win framing.

## Tactics extracted
1. **Mashup ideation** — combine two separately-proven high-view formats into one title. NEW mechanic → feed candidates into the 9-test topic scorer.
2. **Delayed algorithm push** — a video dead at week 1 got pushed weeks later with zero changes. NEW upload-practice intel: don't repackage/judge before ~4-6 weeks.
3. **Edit polish is the biggest quality lever** ($1,600 vs $250 editor was the visible difference: whips, zooms, text). CONFIRM → edit_grammar_ruleset.
4. **Niche deep-watch before producing** (day 2 spent decoding top tier-list videos). CONFIRM → reference deep-watch practice.
5. **AI script ≈ human script, but human was "more concise, just gave the facts."** CONFIRM → 95+ script bar; add a conciseness pass on AI drafts.
6. **Tier-list thumbnail comp** (2 big + 1 worse in middle). Niche-specific; CONTRADICTS our one-focal-point recipe — only relevant if we test a ranking format.
7. **Reference-image-guided AI thumbnail** beat the paid designer. Nuance vs our gen-from-scratch recipe → cheap A/B test.
8. **"Bed test"** — review the near-final cut on a phone, in viewing conditions. Free addition to frame-level QA.
9. **Paid Promotions benchmark** — $0.53/sub, $0.14/view; only edge case = final subs for YPP threshold. IGNORE (spend rule + his own data).

## Policy notes
No YPP/inauthentic-content policy discussion. Implicit signals: an AI-scripted channel was eventually pushed by the algorithm (no suppression observed), and Promotions-bought subscribers appear to count toward monetization thresholds (his stated edge case). ## What we apply
- **All channels**: mashup ideation into the topic scorer; 4-6-week patience rule before repackaging; phone "bed test" added to pre-publish QA.
- **Rexcaped**: one cheap test of a ranking/tier packaging variant ("could you survive X — ranked") using the existing asset library; A/B a reference-guided thumbnail against the from-scratch recipe.
- **Prehistoric POV**: patience rule for the one published video — no premature repackaging.
- **Bizdoc**: conciseness pass on AI-drafted scripts (cut rambling side-facts); ignore Promotions spend.

## Verdict
Mid-value. The experiment is broken (aged vs new channel), the conclusion is course marketing, but the Promotions benchmark, the delayed-push observation, and the mashup-ideation mechanic are real, specific, and free to use.
