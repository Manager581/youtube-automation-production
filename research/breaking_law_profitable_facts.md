## 1. FORD PINTO

### Deaths
- NHTSA data (1971-1977): 27 deaths from 38 rear-end collision fires, 24 non-fatal burn injuries
- Mother Jones estimate (widely cited but disputed): 500-900 burn deaths
- Use "at least 27 confirmed deaths" for defensible claim; "estimates range up to 500" for dramatic range

### The Memo (Grush/Saunby Report)
- **Authors**: E.S. Grush and C.S. Saunby, Ford Environmental and Safety Engineering division
- **Year**: 1973, submitted to NHTSA
- **Cost-benefit conclusion**: Fix cost $11 per vehicle x 12.5 million vehicles = $137 million. Estimated "benefit" of NOT fixing: $49.5 million (180 burn deaths at $200,000 each + 180 burn injuries at $67,000 each + 2,100 burned vehicles at $700 each)
- Ford concluded it was cheaper to let people die ($49.5M) than to fix the car ($137M)
- **What happened to authors**: The memo became public during litigation. Grush and Saunby were Ford engineers — no individual criminal charges were ever filed against them

### Grimshaw v. Ford Motor Co. (1981)
- **Incident**: 1972 rear-end collision in San Bernardino, California
- **Victims**: Driver Lilly Gray (killed), passenger Richard Grimshaw (13 years old, severely burned over entire body, lost portions of fingers on left hand and left ear, underwent numerous skin grafts)
- **Jury verdict (Feb 1978)**: $125 million punitive damages + $2.841 million compensatory to Grimshaw + $665,000 compensatory to Gray family = **$128.5 million total**
- **Judge reduced**: Punitive damages cut from $125M to $3.5 million
- **Appellate court (May 1981)**: Affirmed the reduction
- **Key fact**: $3.5M was STILL the largest punitive damage award in California at the time by a factor of five

### Criminal Trial
- Ford Motor Company was indicted on three counts of reckless homicide in Indiana (1978) — first time a corporation faced criminal homicide charges
- Ford was acquitted in 1980

## 2. META / FACEBOOK

### Cambridge Analytica Timeline
- **2013**: Aleksandr Kogan (Cambridge University data scientist) developed the Facebook app "This Is Your Digital Life" through his company Global Science Research (GSR)
- **2014**: ~270,000 people downloaded the app (personality quiz). Due to Facebook's API permissions at the time, the app harvested data from those users AND all their Facebook friends
- **87 million users' data harvested** (Facebook's own admission, April 2018)
- **2014**: Facebook changed API rules to limit friend-data access, but did NOT enforce deletion of already-harvested data
- **December 2015**: The Guardian's Harry Davies first reported the data collection
- **March 16-17, 2018**: Facebook suspended Cambridge Analytica. The Guardian and NYT published simultaneous investigations
- **Data was used by**: Cambridge Analytica for political ad targeting, including the 2016 Trump campaign and Brexit

### FTC Fine
- **Amount**: $5 billion (exact)
- **Announced**: July 24, 2019
- **FTC vote**: 3-2 (party-line, Republicans for, Democrats against — dissenters wanted to sue, not settle)
- **Largest FTC fine in history** at the time

### Stock Price on Fine Day
- **July 12, 2019** (day fine was first LEAKED): Facebook stock ROSE ~1%, adding ~$10 billion in market cap
- **July 24, 2019** (day fine was officially announced): Stock rose again ~1.1%, adding ~$6 billion to market cap, bringing it over $584 billion
- **The fine MADE Facebook richer**: The market had priced in a potentially larger fine. When $5B was confirmed, investors were RELIEVED
- Stock climbed 22% for the full year 2019

### Zuckerberg Personal Wealth
- Zuckerberg owns ~88.1% of Facebook voting shares (via dual-class stock)
- On the day the fine leaked (July 12), Zuckerberg personally gained ~$1.1 billion as stock rose
- His net worth increased by $13.6 billion in January 2019 alone
- **Key narrative**: The CEO personally gained MORE money from the fine announcement than the fine cost the company

### GDPR / Ireland Fine (separate from FTC)
- **Amount**: EUR 1.2 billion ($1.42 billion) — largest GDPR fine EVER
- **Date**: May 2023
- **Issuer**: Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC)
- **Violation**: Transferring European users' personal data to the United States without adequate safeguards (Standard Contractual Clauses deemed insufficient against US surveillance laws)
- **What Meta changed**: Adopted the new EU-US Data Privacy Framework after the fine

### Users Affected Globally
- Cambridge Analytica: 87 million users (Facebook's admission)
- GDPR Ireland case: affected ALL EU Facebook users (hundreds of millions)

## 3. BOEING 737 MAX

### Crash Dates and Death Tolls
- **Lion Air Flight 610**: October 29, 2018 — 189 killed (all on board)
- **Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302**: March 10, 2019 — 157 killed (149 passengers + 8 crew)
- **Combined**: 346 people killed

### MCAS (Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System)
- Software system that automatically pushed the plane's nose down based on a SINGLE angle-of-attack sensor
- Designed to compensate for the larger engines changing the plane's aerodynamics
- **Pilots were not told about MCAS** — Boeing got FAA to approve removing references to MCAS from the flight manual in 2016
- When the single sensor malfunctioned, MCAS repeatedly forced the nose down; pilots couldn't override it
- Only AFTER Lion Air crash did Boeing instruct pilots on corrective action

### "Designed by Clowns" Emails
- **Released**: January 2020 (Boeing was compelled to release internal communications to Congress)
- **Key quote** (2016, a Boeing technical pilot to a colleague): "This airplane is designed by clowns, who in turn are supervised by monkeys"
- Other messages showed Boeing employees mocking FAA oversight and discussing efforts to minimize simulator training requirements to save airlines money
- Employees described hiding simulator problems from the FAA

### Dennis Muilenburg's Exit
- **Fired**: December 23, 2019
- **Severance**: Boeing said he received NO severance payment
- **BUT**: He kept already-vested stock options, pension, and deferred compensation
- **Total payout estimates**: $62 million (NBC/CBC), $80.7 million (Bloomberg/Fortune), ~$60 million (CNBC)
- **Most cited figure**: ~$62 million walking away, no severance but massive accumulated compensation
- **Contrast**: Families of crash victims received $144,500 per person from Boeing's initial $50M fund (per ABC News)

### FAA Self-Certification (ODA Program)
- FAA's Organization Designation Authorization (ODA) program let Boeing certify its own aircraft
- **By 2018, Boeing was certifying 96% of its own work** (only 4% directly checked by FAA)
- Designated organizations at four US aircraft manufacturers approved ~94% of certification activities
- Created in 2005, the ODA program let companies conduct repairs, modifications, and inspections WITHOUT FAA approval
- House Committee conclusion: The crashes were "the horrific culmination" of "grossly insufficient oversight by the FAA — the pernicious result of regulatory capture"
- **Reform**: 2020 Aircraft Certification, Safety, and Accountability Act mandated sweeping reforms

## 4. WELLS FARGO

### Fake Accounts
- **Initial disclosure (2016)**: ~2 million unauthorized accounts (1.5 million checking/savings + 500,000+ credit cards)
- **Updated figure**: 3.5 million fake accounts (after internal review expanded the scope)
- **5,300 employees fired** for fraudulent sales practices (2011-2016)

### Cross-Selling Pressure System
- **Mantra**: "Eight is Great" — every customer should have 8 products with Wells Fargo
- Employees had daily/weekly sales quotas that were impossible to meet legitimately
- Those who didn't meet quotas faced demotion or termination
- Employees opened accounts in customers' names, moved money between accounts, created fake email addresses, and ordered credit cards customers never requested

### John Stumpf's Exit
- **Resigned**: October 2016
- **Initial clawback**: $41 million in unvested stock awards
- **Total clawback**: $69 million across multiple actions
- **What he could have left with**: ~$200 million in cash, stock, and options (per CNN analysis)
- **SEC charges (Nov 2020)**: Charged with misleading investors; settled for $2.5 million civil penalty + officer/director bar

### Carrie Tolstedt (Head of Community Banking)
- **Original exit package**: $124.6 million in stock and options
- **Total clawed back**: $67 million
- **SEC settlement**: $4.9 million civil penalty
- **Criminal charge**: Pled guilty to obstruction of a bank examination (March 2023)
- **Sentence (Sept 15, 2023)**: 3 years probation + 6 months home confinement — **NO PRISON TIME**
- **Additional penalty**: Lifetime banking industry ban + $17 million OCC civil penalty
- **Key narrative**: The ONLY Wells Fargo executive criminally charged. Got zero prison time.

### Fines/Penalties Against Wells Fargo (Corporate)
- 2016: $185 million (CFPB + OCC + LA City Attorney)
- 2018: $1 billion (CFPB + OCC)
- 2020: $3 billion (DOJ + SEC combined settlement)

## 5. PURDUE PHARMA / SACKLERS

### Wealth Transfers Before Bankruptcy
- **$10.7 billion** withdrawn from Purdue by the Sackler family (per December 2019 AlixPartners audit)
- Transfers occurred after the company began receiving legal scrutiny
- **$4 billion** transferred between 2008-2016 (per Massachusetts lawsuit)
- Family discussed litigation exposure as early as 2007 — a full decade before the major legal attacks began
- Richard Sackler email (2014) to his sons: acknowledged shareholders "took the money out of the business" during years of "massive amounts of cash"

### Richard Sackler's "Blizzard" Email
- **1996 OxyContin launch event**: Richard Sackler told the sales force: "The launch of OxyContin Tablets will be followed by a blizzard of prescriptions that will bury the competition."
- He served as president of Purdue Pharma (1999-2003) and chairman

### Kermit, West Virginia Pharmacy
- **Sav-Rite Pharmacy** in Kermit, WV — population ~400
- **9 million opioid pills** shipped to the town over a 2-year period
- That equals ~11,000 pills per resident per year
- Pharmacy filled 600-1,000 prescriptions daily, over 90% for hydrocodone
- **Shut down 2011**, owner sent to prison for fraud
- Distributors (including McKesson) shipped 5+ million pills to this single pharmacy

### Settlement
- **$7.4 billion** total settlement (finalized 2025)
- Sackler family pays up to $6.5 billion over 15 years
- Purdue pays nearly $900 million on court approval
- All 55 attorneys general signed on

### Total Opioid Deaths (Cumulative US)
- **~806,000 opioid-specific overdose deaths** from 1999-2023 (CDC)
- **~1.3 million total drug overdose deaths** since 1999 (all drugs, not just opioids)
- 2024 data: opioid deaths dropped to ~54,743 (down from 83,140 in 2023)
- **Best defensible figure for script**: "More than 800,000 Americans have died from opioid overdoses since 1999"

## 6. REALPAGE

### ProPublica Investigation
- **Published**: October 2022
- Revealed RealPage's revenue management software was enabling landlord price coordination
- Legal experts said it could result in "cartel-like behavior"

### How It Worked
- RealPage's algorithm ingested nonpublic, competitively sensitive lease data from competing landlords
- Used this to calibrate rental price recommendations
- Software included features designed to restrain price decreases and steer pricing alignment
- Hosted meetings where competitors shared sensitive insights
- **13 million+ rental units** in the database

### DOJ Settlement
- **Filed**: November 2025 (proposed settlement, pending judicial approval)
- DOJ sued six of the nation's largest landlords alongside RealPage
- The six landlord defendants operate **1.3 million rental units in 43 states**
- **Key terms**:
  - RealPage must stop using competitors' nonpublic data in its revenue management product
  - Model training limited to historic data at least 12 months old
  - Must remove mechanisms that prop up prices or encourage common pricing ranges
  - Users must be able to set prices BELOW previously defined "floors"
- **After ProPublica's story**: 24+ federal lawsuits filed by renters alleging antitrust violations
- RealPage then sued New York state over its ban on algorithmic rent-setting

### Tenant Stories
- No specific "Marquita Davis" story found in search results. ProPublica's investigation focused on systemic impact rather than individual named tenants in their primary reporting.

## 7. REDDIT vs PERPLEXITY

### The Trap Post
- Reddit created a post visible only to Google (not regular Reddit users)
- Within hours, the post appeared in Perplexity's search results — proving Perplexity was scraping Reddit content

### Cease-and-Desist Timeline
- **May 2024**: Reddit sent Perplexity a cease-and-desist letter
- **After receiving the C&D**: Perplexity INCREASED citations to Reddit content **forty-fold** (40x)
- **October 22, 2025**: Reddit filed federal lawsuit in Southern District of New York

### The Lawsuit
- Defendants: Perplexity AI, SerpApi, Oxylabs UAB, AWM Proxy
- Allegations: DMCA anti-circumvention violations, unjust enrichment, unfair competition
- Defendants allegedly masked identities, rotated IP addresses, bypassed access controls to scrape "billions" of Google SERPs containing Reddit content
- Reddit seeks monetary damages, disgorgement of profits, and injunctive relief
- **Specific damages amount**: Not publicly specified in filings found (the $1.6B figure could not be verified)

## 8. AI SCRAPING LAWSUITS

### Total Count (as of late 2025/early 2026)
- **70+ AI copyright infringement lawsuits** filed by copyright owners (per Copyright Alliance)
- **51 detailed tracked cases** as of October 2025
- **3 judges** have ruled on fair use so far: 2 found fair use, 1 found infringement
- Next batch of fair use rulings expected summer 2026

### Anthropic / Shadow Libraries
- Anthropic downloaded **7 million+ copyrighted books** from shadow libraries (LibGen, Pirate Library Mirror/PiLiMi)
- **June 2025**: Judge William Alsup ruled using legally acquired books for training = fair use, BUT downloading pirated copies = NOT fair use (likely infringement)
- **September 2025**: Anthropic settled for **$1.5 billion** (Bartz v. Anthropic)
- Largest copyright settlement in history
- Covered 482,460 books; ~$3,000 per work
- Fairness hearing: April 2026
- Filed by Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber, Kirk Wallace Johnson

### OpenAI / NYT Case
- **Filed**: December 2023 by The New York Times
- Consolidated with 16 other copyright lawsuits
- **20 million ChatGPT conversation logs** ordered produced:
  - NYT initially requested 120 million logs (from tens of billions)
  - OpenAI countered with 20 million (0.5% of total)
  - October 2025: OpenAI tried to change to keyword-only search
  - November 2025: Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang rejected keyword approach
  - **January 5, 2026**: Judge Sidney Stein affirmed order — OpenAI must produce full 20M-log sample (de-identified)
- Case is ongoing; no trial date set

### Sarah Silverman Cases
- **Filed July 2023**: Two lawsuits — one against OpenAI, one against Meta
- **Against OpenAI**: Most claims dismissed February 2024 (no "substantial similarity" in outputs). Core copyright claim survived. Discovery ongoing (deadline April 2025).
- **Against Meta**: Key market harm issue ruled on June 2025. Core claims survived. Case ongoing.
- **Neither case fully dismissed** — both are still active as of early 2026

### OpenAI Shadow Libraries
- OpenAI used training datasets called "books 1" and "books 2" — downloaded from LibGen (shadow library)
- OpenAI later deleted the datasets but the fact of their use became evidence in litigation
