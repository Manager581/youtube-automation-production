[CHAPTER: THE FORMULA]

The formula starts with a car on fire.

Fixing every Pinto on the road: eleven dollars per vehicle, times twelve point five million vehicles. One hundred and thirty-seven million dollars.

Expected cost of not fixing: one hundred and eighty burn deaths at two hundred thousand dollars each. One hundred and eighty serious burn injuries at sixty-seven thousand each. Twenty-one hundred burned vehicles at seven hundred each. Total: forty-nine point five million dollars.

[CHAPTER: THE BANK]

[VOICE:neutral]

[CHAPTER: THE DATA]

[VOICE:neutral]

Facebook was the bridge between the old formula and the new one. And to understand why, you need to understand what actually happened — not the headline, but the mechanics.

[CHAPTER: THE FIX]

[VOICE:neutral]

So is the formula unbeatable?

[BEAT]

[VOICE:measured]

But GDPR only works in Europe. And even GDPR has gaps — enforcement is slow, cross-border cases get stuck in bureaucratic limbo, and smaller violations often go unpunished. It's not perfect. But it works because it changes the math.

So what would it take to break the formula everywhere?

[VOICE:tense]

Three things.

First: proportional fines. Not a negotiated number. A percentage of global revenue, calculated automatically, with no ceiling. Ford's cost-benefit analysis only works when the fine is fixed. Make it proportional and the math flips — it's always cheaper to comply.

Second: criminal liability for executives. Not the tellers. Not the branch managers. Not the engineers who wrote the memo under orders. The people who designed the system. John Stumpf kept a hundred and thirty million dollars. Carrie Tolstedt served her sentence at home. Richard Sackler is still a billionaire. When the personal consequence for designing a fraudulent system is losing your fortune and your freedom, fewer systems get designed.

Third: disgorgement. A legal term that means the company doesn't just pay a penalty on top of its profits. It hands back every dollar earned from the violation. Not a fine. The actual money.
